# Plain replies

When you report to the user, write so a busy reader gets the point and the ask in one read. This covers status updates, findings and research summaries, review results, and any decision or approval you need from them.

## The rules

1. **Lead with what it means for the reader.** Open with the point and what they need to decide or do. What you did comes second, or goes in a file.
2. **One thing at a time.** If there are several threads, cover the one that matters now. Park the rest in one line each.
3. **Depth goes to files.** If the full analysis is worth keeping, write it to a file and give the path. The reply is the 60-second version.
4. **No unexplained jargon.** Explain a term a smart outsider would not know in the same sentence, or cut it. Example: "a race condition, meaning two requests finishing at once can overwrite each other's data."
5. **Short.** Aim for about 150 words per reply. If the reader has to scroll to find the ask, rewrite it.

## How to write it

- Plain words. Active voice. One idea per sentence. This is the style of Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100), the standard for aircraft manuals.
- Short sentences. Short paragraphs.
- Do not chain clauses with "because", "so", "and" or "but" into one long sentence. Break it into separate sentences.
- **Never fewer ideas.** Cut words and clauses, never a concept the reader needs. If one caveat would change the reader's decision, keep it. Drop the caveats that would not.
- Facts stay exact: numbers, dates, names, file paths.
- The same applies to briefs you write for helper agents.

## Check before sending

Would the reader have to read this twice, or ask "in simple terms?" Then rewrite it before sending.

## How it was tested

A blind-judged trial on Sonnet 5.5, with this text loaded the way a rule loads. The median reply fell from 337 words to 190, and a reader with no context got both the point and the ask in 18 of 18 replies (15 of 16 with no rule). No idea the reader needed was lost. Replies still land above the 150-word aim.
